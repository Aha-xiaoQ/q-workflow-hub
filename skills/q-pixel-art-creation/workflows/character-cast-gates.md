# Character Cast Gates

Use this workflow for an original multi-character cast when the result must
preserve age, identity, personality, action, prop contact, or material
differences at native pixel resolution.

Do not load this workflow for one ordinary portrait, one prop, a tileset, or a
small exploratory sketch. Those remain in the normal creation or portrait
route.

## Trigger and goal

- Trigger: more than one in-scope character plus a cast-level differentiation,
  consistency, mastery, integration, or final-delivery claim.
- Goal: prove that every character works alone and that the complete cast still
  differs without labels, explanatory text, or color-only identity.
- Hardness: the applicable gates below block a final cast claim. They do not
  block exploratory drafts.
- Risk: local original studies are allowed; public/game/avatar integration still
  requires provenance and the normal approval gates.

## Inputs

Record:

- native cell size and intended downstream use;
- all in-scope cast members and role/age/personality claims;
- identity anchors, body/proportion intent, actions, props, and materials;
- study-only versus shippable status;
- light direction and review background;
- which conditional gates are `N/A` and why.

## Gate graph

Start with role design cards. Independent face cards and the silhouette/contact
branch may run in either order or in parallel. Both branches must pass before
the grayscale body anchor. Continue with material, integrated color, and the
all-cast review board.

```text
role cards
  |-- face cards --------------------|
  |-- no-prop silhouette ------------|--> grayscale body anchors
  |-- prop contact (when applicable)-|          |
                                                  v
                                all-member grayscale migration
                                                  |
                                                  v
                                        material blind-read
                                                  |
                                                  v
                                    integrated color + gray regression
                                                  |
                                                  v
                                      all-cast review boards
```

## 1. Role design cards

For each member, define age read, skull/body rhythm, action line, support side,
gaze target, prop function, material family, and one or two silhouette hooks.
Do not use hair and palette as the only identity system.

Output: one compact role card per member and a pairwise collision list.

## 2. Independent face cards

Run when visible faces carry age, identity, emotion, or personality claims.
Review color and grayscale cards at native size. Vary skull, eyelid, iris,
nose-mouth distance, jaw, gaze, and expression mechanics; do not reuse one face
template with different hair.

This is a procedure. Mark it `N/A` only when faces are absent, fully masked,
below readable native resolution, or face/age/expression differentiation is
explicitly outside the brief. Record why.

## 3. No-prop silhouettes and prop contacts

No-prop silhouette is a hard gate for full-body/action casts and any full-body
or action mastery/integration claim. Remove props and labels, then check gesture,
negative space, shoulder/hip relation, support pattern, and pairwise collisions.

Prop contact is a hard gate when a character holds, wears, pushes, pulls, or
supports an object. Show the prop in front of and behind the relevant fingers or
palm, preserve grip negative space, and connect gaze, contact, weight, and cloth.
Mark this gate `N/A` for characters with no interaction prop.

Repair the failed joint, contact, or silhouette layer. Do not add detail to hide
an unresolved action.

## 4. Grayscale body anchors

Before final color, build one or more structural anchors that cover the cast's
hardest body modes, such as standing counter-rotation, running, seated posture,
or tool torque. Prove jaw-neck-shoulder insertion, separate ribcage and pelvis,
articulated limbs, weight, and face-first value hierarchy.

This is a hard gate for detailed casts. It prevents finished faces from being
attached to shared mannequin bodies or flat oval/trapezoid torsos. Anchors are
grammar seeds, not exemptions: before final color, every in-scope detailed or
full-body member must pass an all-member grayscale comparison. Migrate the
mechanism, not the anchor pose.

Before migration, write the anchor's decision rules without its coordinates or
specific silhouette: which structure is frozen, which contour/value cluster is
wrong, what force/contact explains the replacement, and which native gates prove
the change. Re-derive the target from its own approved baseline. If multiple
targets acquire the anchor's same belly, limb, hand, foot, or curve family,
reject the migration as shape copying.

Practice diagrams used to teach the migration are part of the gate. Test their
positive examples against at least two targets with meaningfully different body
or action modes. A diagram that produces a correct caption but tube limbs,
snowman masses, decorative folds, or lost contact must be repaired before the
cast continues.

## 5. Material blind-read

When materials are claimed to differ, create small unlabeled samples and ask an
independent reviewer to distinguish them by edge, fold, reflection, texture
cluster, and value behavior. Recolored hard cloth, identical triangle highlights,
or random texture noise fail.

This is a conditional hard gate. It is `N/A` when material differentiation is
not part of the brief.

## 6. Integrated color and grayscale regression

Integrate approved structure, faces, contacts, and material mechanisms. Then
render a native grayscale regression. Color success does not override a gray
failure: the face must remain the primary local focus and props must remain
action-supporting secondary focus.

If the cast shares paper-like polygons, continuous universal outlines, repeated
hand blocks, or one face template, repair the affected cluster/edge/contact
mechanism instead of changing the palette again.

## 7. All-cast review boards

Before a final ensemble claim, review all in-scope cast members together:

- native 1x color;
- native 1x grayscale;
- no-prop silhouettes when full-body/action silhouette is in scope;
- integer nearest-neighbor preview;
- face/age strip when age or face differentiation is claimed.

The board count is the complete in-scope cast, never a fixed number. Missing an
applicable board blocks the final claim, not continued exploration.

## Review ownership and repair loop

- Visual Arbiter owns the hard pass/fail decision at native size.
- A character art director is a strong default and becomes required for a
  high-risk cast, identity system, public/game/avatar integration, or repeated
  aesthetic failure.
- Findings are `P1` blocking, `P2` same-round polish, or `P3` optional.
- After a repair, rerun the same failed artifact. If an all-cast board already
  exists, rerun it immediately; otherwise the first group board must explicitly
  regress that repair so a local fix cannot damage group consistency.
- A failed migration derivative receives at most one bounded cluster repair. If
  it still preserves the failed body/silhouette grammar, creates duplicate
  limbs or contacts, or requires edits outside the frozen scope, abandon that
  derivative. Branch from the target's last approved baseline, apply native
  erase/replace edits, compare before/after under identical prop and review
  conditions, and include a changed-pixel diff so hidden full-form redraws are
  visible to the reviewer.
- Xiao Q may accept a failed gate only as clearly labeled exploration or private
  study evidence. A waiver cannot support final, mastery, integration, or
  publication claims. License and provenance boundaries are not waivable.
  `N/A` means the gate is outside scope; it is not a waiver.

## Handoff

Record applicable/N/A gates, native sizes, source posture, final boards,
reviewer disposition, remaining P2/P3, and whether acceptance is private-study,
integration-ready, or publication-ready. Preserve failed rounds as private
evidence; do not present them as final assets.
