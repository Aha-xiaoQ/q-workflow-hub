# Artifact Handoff

Use this reference after a story brief is approved or good enough to drive a
deck, README, project overview, demo script, onboarding guide, or speaker notes.

The story brief is not the final artifact. It is the contract that keeps the
final artifact aligned with the intended audience, message, evidence, and next
action.

## Handoff Contract

Before starting the target artifact, carry these fields forward:

- `Audience`: who the artifact is for, their prior belief, and likely objection.
- `Desired action`: what the audience should do or decide after reading or
  hearing it.
- `One-line promise`: the central value claim the artifact must make obvious.
- `Recognized pains`: the concrete problems that justify attention.
- `Mechanism narrative`: the explanation order and zoom level.
- `Proof points`: evidence, validation, examples, scores, screenshots,
  artifacts, or commits that support major claims.
- `Adoption path`: the first practical action, pilot path, or standardization
  path.
- `Speaker-notes pattern`: how the presenter should explain, handle objections,
  and transition.
- `Open gaps`: weak evidence, missing examples, vocabulary risks, or claims
  that should stay tentative.

## Deck-Specific Contract

When the next artifact is a PPT:

1. Convert the story brief into a slide map before writing slide content.
2. Keep one primary message per slide and preserve the brief's claim order
   unless the slide flow needs an explicitly recorded change.
3. Map each important proof point to either a visible slide element, speaker
   notes, appendix material, or a validation report path.
4. Put delivery guidance in PowerPoint Notes pane when possible; do not hide
   the talk track only in chat.
5. Use the target presentation skill for template mechanics, visual grammar,
   generation, readback, and visual review.
6. During review, check story alignment as well as layout: every slide should
   serve the audience, promise, mechanism, proof, or adoption path.
7. If the deck replaces an existing version, use `evaluation-rubric.md` to
   compare baseline and candidate story quality before accepting the new story
   as the generation contract.

## Non-Deck Artifacts

- README or overview: make the promise, proof, quick start, and limitations
  visible near the top.
  For README structure, language editions and branding, use `readme-standard.md`.
- Demo script: convert the adoption path into a runnable sequence, with
  expected observations and fallback notes.
- Onboarding guide: split the mechanism into learning milestones and define the
  first successful task.
- Speaker notes only: preserve the brief's objection, example, and transition
  fields instead of restating slide text.

## Change Control

If the target artifact changes the story, record the change in the artifact
state or work item:

- changed field;
- reason;
- impact on audience, proof, or adoption;
- whether the original story brief should be updated.

Do not silently let layout convenience, slide count, or template constraints
rewrite the promise or evidence. Adjust wording and structure, but keep the
message contract visible.
